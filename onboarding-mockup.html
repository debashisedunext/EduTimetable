01

Two kinds of customer

They buy different things, arrive by different roads, and disagree about something fundamental: who owns the school and its people. Everything below follows from that one difference.

🆕 Bought the Timetable module only
  1. A stranger finds the home page and presses Create account.
  2. They give their own details — name, work email, password, organisation.
  3. They verify the email and sign in. They own no school yet.
  4. They land on My Schools and create one — they type the name, because nobody else knows it. They may create several.
  5. They run the wizard, then create their own users and generate teacher logins, so staff can sign in and see their own timetable, reports and substitutions.
Owns everything: the school, the staff list and the logins. All editable.
🔗 Bought the ERP — SSO, unchanged
  1. They click Timetable inside the ERP. A signed, single-use token arrives carrying the school's code and name.
  2. The school is provisioned on the spot if unknown; the name is refreshed every login.
  3. Step 1 shows those details already filled in — read, press Next. A trust gets a picker from the token's schools[].
  4. No school can be added here. Schools exist because the ERP says so.
  5. No user can be added here either. Every staff member appears the first time they open Timetable from the ERP, with the role their ERP role maps to.
Owns nothing here: schools, staff and logins all come from the ERP. Read-only.

Both roads meet at the same place

From the moment a school is selected, there is one onboarding — the same three doors, the same eleven steps, the same commit — and afterwards one app, one permission registry and one set of scoped views. The lanes differ only in where identity came from.

The same split, in three places — so make it one mechanism

School identity, the school list and now the user list all need to be read-only for ERP customers and editable for self-serve ones. Three screens each deciding that for themselves is three chances to get it wrong. One column — schools.origin — decides all three, and any future screen inherits the answer for free.

02

Eight things to settle before any code

The first two are new in this revision and are the serious ones — each contradicts a rule the system currently states outright. Neither is a blocker; both change what gets built.

① Local login contradicts a stated invariant, and adds real attack surface

The project currently says, in CLAUDE.md invariant 16: "SSO-only entry… there is no local login". Self-serve signup makes that false. users has no password column, and is keyed (school_id, erp_user_id) — an ERP-identity table, not a credential store.

Proposed: credentials live once, in the control plane — a new accounts table beside platform_users, never inside a tenant database. One account, many schools, one password. Each school still gets its ordinary users row, linked by a stable identity string, so every existing permission, scope and audit path keeps working unchanged. SSO users get no account row and no password; the two identity sources never mix.

This brings work that SSO let us skip entirely: Argon2id hashing, email verification before first sign-in, password reset with single-use expiring tokens, rate limiting, and login responses that do not reveal whether an address is registered. That is sub-phase 25.0 below, and it is the part I would not compress.

② A login may create a school — but never a database

§17.3 says provisioning "is an operator command, never something a login triggers", and in the control plane Tenant.erp_instance_id is required — a self-serve school has no ERP instance to point at.

Proposed, and the distinction is the whole answer: a self-serve school is a row in the shared tenant group — a schools record plus a tenants entry with mode = shared. That is cheap, instant and safe. A dedicated database stays exactly what it is today: an operator command (pnpm tenant:create), never reachable from a sign-up form. So the sentence becomes "creating a database is an operator command", which is the part that actually mattered. erp_instance_id becomes nullable.

It also needs a ceiling: an unverified account creating schools in a loop is a trivial denial of service. Verified email required, and a per-account cap (say 10) that an operator can raise.

③ Only an admin creates schools. A teacher signs in to look, and to nothing else.

Settled. Two rules, enforced in two different places, because they are two different questions.

Who may create a school — accounts.kind: an owner registered themselves from the home page and may create schools; a member was invited into one and may never create any. Teachers are always members. Same login screen, same credential machinery, one field's difference — and My Schools simply has no "Add a school" tile for a member. POST /schools refuses a member outright, in the server, because hiding the tile is cosmetic.

What a teacher may do inside a school — the existing Teacher role, unchanged: timetable.view.own, timetable.view.class, reports.view, notifications.view. Four permissions, none of which writes anything. No generate, no drag-and-drop, no publish, no master data, no roles, no AI configuration. And the §15 rule that makes this real rather than cosmetic: the view levels are row-level scope filters injected server-side into every query — REST, sockets, reports and AI tools alike — so a teacher gets their own grid and their linked sections whatever they send.

③b One thing to confirm: substitution entry

Your earlier brief said teachers could log in to see "timetable, reports, substitution entry if any". This one says "no changes they can be able to do". Substitution entry is a write, so the two do not agree.

I have taken the stricter reading: the default Teacher role is view-only, and there is no pre-made "Teacher + Substitutions" role. If you do want some teachers to record cover, it needs no new permission and no new build — substitute.manage already exists and the Roles & Responsibility screen already lets an admin tick it onto a role. So this stays a decision you can make later, on a screen that already ships, rather than a thing to design now.

④ A locally-created user still needs a stable identity string

users is unique on (school_id, erp_user_id) and that column is NOT NULL — it is how every session, audit row and scope filter finds a person. A user created on our own Users screen has no ERP id.

Proposed: write a synthetic, stable value — local:{account_id} — and add a nullable users.account_id pointing at the control-plane account. The unique key, the session token and every existing query keep working untouched. The column name then slightly overstates itself; renaming it to external_user_id is a clean but wide change (auth, SSO, platform admin, the isolation sweep) and I would do it separately rather than inside this phase.

⑤ School identity now has two provenances, and they must not be confused

An ERP school's name is the ERP's and is overwritten on every login — letting someone edit it would produce an edit that silently reverts. A self-serve school's name is the admin's and must be editable.

Proposed: schools.origin — erp or self_serve. Step 1 and the School Profile screen read it: erp renders read-only with "managed by your ERP", self_serve renders an editable field. One column decides it everywhere, rather than each screen guessing.

⑥ "Popup on every login" becomes something people dismiss unread

Proposed: the three doors open by themselves only while the school has no timetable config — the real definition of "new" — and afterwards live behind a permanent Set up a timetable button. For a self-serve admin who has just created a school, that condition is true by construction, so it opens immediately anyway.

⑦ A single slider cannot describe a Secondary wing

9–12 with one handle means creating Pre-Nur→12 and then deleting nine classes. Proposed: a two-handle range. The Primary case never moves the left handle, so nothing is lost. Mocked below — drag it.

⑧ The assistant stays an interviewer, not a second writer

Option 3's steps 1–8 create academic years, timetable configs and rooms — all deliberately absent from what the assistant may draft (§13.5). Proposed: the AI asks the questions and fills the same wizard state Option 2 fills; pressing Create runs the identical commit. Zero new authority, one commit path, and Option 3 becomes cheap because it reuses everything.

The constraint that governs all of it: no second write path

06

What local login costs

SSO let us skip all of this, because the ERP owned the credential. Owning it ourselves means owning these seven things, and none is optional.

Argon2id, never bcrypt-by-habit

Memory-hard, tuned to ~100 ms on the API container, parameters stored beside the hash so they can be raised later without a reset.

Verify before first sign-in

Unverified accounts cannot create a school. Otherwise a typo'd address creates schools nobody can reach, and a script creates thousands.

No account enumeration

Wrong email and wrong password give the same message and take the same time. Registration and password-reset say "check your email" whether or not the address exists.

Reset tokens are single-use

Hashed at rest, short expiry, invalidated on use and on any password change — so an old link in an inbox is inert.

Throttle both axes

Per address and per source IP. One catches credential stuffing against one victim; the other catches spraying across many.

One session token, two issuers

Password login issues exactly the JWT the SSO callback issues. Every downstream guard, row-scope filter and audit path stays as it is — this is what keeps the blast radius inside auth.

A school cap with an operator override

Per account, default 10. A verified human rarely needs more; a script always does.

Audit both paths identically

Register, verify, login, failed login, reset and school-create all land in the audit log — so "who created this school" has an answer.

Isolation gate covers the new routes

Public routes must be explicitly classified as unauthenticated in the §17.8 sweep — a new controller fails the build until somebody decides which, which is exactly the point.

07

How it gets proved

The end-to-end test that matters

scripts/onboarding-smoke.cjs: register an account, verify it, sign in, create a school, drive all eleven steps through the API, and assert the school reaches 100% Readiness and generates a conflict-free timetable — from a stranger on the home page to a working timetable, without one row typed by hand. If that path cannot produce a solvable school, it has not worked however good it looks.

Two accounts, two schools, no leakage

Account A creates school 1, account B creates school 2; assert B is refused A's school and gets a different answer for its own. A route that refuses everyone proves nothing and is reported as such (§17.8).

The auth negatives

Unverified account cannot create a school; reset token works once and never again; a stale token after a password change is dead; unknown email and wrong password are indistinguishable in body and in timing; the cap actually caps.

Resume, honestly

Abandon at step 6, sign in again, assert every earlier answer returns and no partial rows were written. A wizard that half-commits is worse than one that loses your work.

SSO is unchanged — prove it

The existing SSO suite must pass untouched, and an ERP school's name must still be overwritten from the token on every login while its Step 1 stays read-only.

A teacher login is a teacher login

Invite a teacher, accept, sign in, and assert they get their own grid and their linked sections only — the existing scope negatives, run against a locally-created user rather than an SSO one. Then assert the same account is refused school creation, and that the invite token is dead the second time.

The ERP customer's screens are inert

With origin = 'erp', assert POST /schools and POST /users/invite are refused by the server, not merely hidden in the UI — the hiding is cosmetic, as §15 has always said.

The suggestions are checked, not admired

Unit tests on the room suggester and curriculum template: labs carry their subject mappings, and no proposed curriculum ever exceeds the wing's weekly capacity — including the 8-period-week case a fixed table would break.

08

Deliberately not in this phase

  • The full marketing home page. Scaffolded here with real structure, palette and type; screenshots, pricing table, testimonials and footer are separate design work, as agreed.
  • Billing and plans. The school cap is a fixed number an operator can raise — not a subscription. "7 remaining on your plan" in the mockup is a label, not a payment system.
  • Social sign-in (Google / Microsoft). A third identity source on top of two is more surface than value right now; the accounts table leaves room for it.
  • Self-registration by staff. A teacher cannot sign up and attach themselves to a school — they are invited, always. Otherwise anyone who guesses a school code is inside it.
  • Changing a user's email. It is their sign-in. Changing it is an account operation with its own verification round-trip, not a dropdown on an admin's grid.
  • Split electives in the wizard. A third-language block has its own screen (§4.9); a twelfth step would make the common path pay for the rare one.
  • Editing an existing school through the wizard. This is a first-run path; pointing it at a published timetable needs a diff-and-merge story that is its own phase.
  • Changing the Setup Wizard. Option 1 is the current process, untouched — which is what makes it a safe fallback from either of the other two.

Rough size

25.0 is the new critical path and the only part I would not compress — it is auth, and auth is where compression shows up later as an incident. 25.1 is small and mostly transactional. 25.2–25.4 are the bulk: eleven steps, two grid components, two suggesters, one migration with solver work behind it. 25.5 stays small because of decision ⑧ — the assistant reuses 25.2–25.4 rather than duplicating it. Cutting 25.5 loses a door, not a foundation.
